Our Mission

Strategic Framework

Awdal Water & Education Initiative Inc. (AWEI) is an independent international non-governmental organization dedicated to mitigating the devastating impacts of climate-induced drought in Awdal. By building resilient water infrastructure and stabilizing local primary schools, we safeguard both community health and the educational future of the region's children.

Core Strategy

Three Pillars of Action

I. WASH

Water Security

AWEI improves local water infrastructure and community health through cleaning and construction initiatives, using cost-effective methods while ensuring long-term sustainability.

II. Education

Educational Access

AWEI uses school-adjacent projects to support educational access and community resilience. The initial pilot will provide need-based nonperishable food assistance to community residents, with particular attention to children and families affected by food insecurity. Future improvements to educational or community facilities remain long-term goals subject to funding and local planning.

III. Community

Community Resilience

AWEI partners directly with local elders, municipal leaders, and regional technicians to ensure long-term community ownership and sustainability.

The Challenge

The Crisis: Water Scarcity & Educational Disruption

Understanding the root causes that drive AWEI's mission across the Awdal region.

Regional Context

A Cycle We Can Break

Extended ecological crises in the Awdal region have disproportionately impacted rural and pastoralist families. When traditional water sources fail, children — particularly young girls — are pulled out of classrooms to walk miles in search of water, or schools are forced to shut down entirely due to unsafe sanitary conditions.

AWEI works to improve community rainwater storage while providing interim household water-treatment support as longer-term solutions are evaluated.

During multi-season droughts, limited water resources create dire conditions that push families toward unsafe water sources and consumption practices. Reliable, clean water infrastructure anchored within communities is what keeps families and schools in place — without it, displacement is inevitable.

Why This Matters
Nearly half of rural households still lack access to basic drinking water services, against 79% coverage in urban areas (UNICEF WASH Needs Assessment, 2024)
Roughly 40% of existing water sources are non-functional — scarcity that pushes families toward unsafe water sources and consumption practices (WHO–UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme)
Households without reliable access to clean water nearly doubled during recent drought years, from 5.6 million to 10.5 million across the Horn of Africa (UNICEF)
Women and girls travel ever-longer distances to collect water, pulling girls from classrooms and exposing them to heightened risk (UNICEF)
Sources: UNICEF WASH Needs Assessment (2024); WHO–UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme; UNICEF Horn of Africa drought reporting. Figures are national and regional; they describe the conditions AWEI's work in Awdal responds to.

Summer 2026

The AWEI Summer 2026 Pilot Campaign

Funding Goal $15,000 USD

AWEI's initial pilot will serve Qolqol in the Awdal region. Subject to fundraising and final engineering estimates, the project will complete an existing community rainwater barkad near the local school, add appropriate safety improvements, provide temporary household water-treatment supplies and instruction, and distribute nonperishable food based on community need. The barkad will be open to the entire community and maintained for public use by community leadership, with oversight and periodic monitoring by AWEI.
